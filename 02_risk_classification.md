# Risk Classification

## EU AI Act

**Classification:** Limited risk (initial assessment)

**Reasoning:** Nora is a banking customer-service chatbot that answers questions about banking services. It does not assess individuals, determine eligibility, or make decisions that affect access to financial services.

**Main obligation:** Users should be informed that they are interacting with an AI system, where the applicable transparency requirements require this.

## Scope Change Trigger

If Nora is later used to assess the creditworthiness of individuals or establish their credit score, its intended purpose could fall under the EU AI Act's high-risk classification for creditworthiness assessment.

This change would require a reassessment of applicable obligations, potentially including risk management, data governance, technical documentation, record-keeping, human oversight, accuracy, and monitoring.

## UK Regulatory Position

The UK does not currently have one comprehensive AI-specific law equivalent to the EU AI Act. Existing laws and sector regulators govern relevant AI uses.

Five cross-sector principles for AI regulation are:

- Safety, security and robustness
- Appropriate transparency and explainability
- Fairness
- Accountability and governance
- Contestability and redress

### Relevant Requirements for a Bank

- **FCA Consumer Duty:** Nora should support good customer outcomes, provide understandable information, avoid foreseeable customer harm, and account for the needs of vulnerable customers.
- **UK GDPR and ICO guidance:** Personal data processed by Nora must be handled lawfully, fairly and transparently, with appropriate security and data minimisation. Relevant data protection rights and safeguards must also be considered.
